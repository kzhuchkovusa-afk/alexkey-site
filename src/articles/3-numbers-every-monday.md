Most owners of learning centers I talk to are in one of two places: thirty reports in their software they never open — or no numbers at all.

Either way, they find out the month was bad when the month is over.

## Where the three numbers come from

When we ran a network of coding schools across 20+ countries, we couldn't visit most locations.

So every Monday, every location sent three numbers:

1. **Trials booked this week.**
2. **Trial → enrollment last week.** How many trials became students.
3. **Students with two absences in a row.**

That's the whole dashboard.

## How to read it

**Number 1 is down → a marketing problem.** Not enough people in the door.

**Number 2 is down → a trial problem.** People come, but don't stay. Fix the trial and the parent conversation.

**Number 3 is up → a retention problem.** Families are leaving quietly.

Each number points to a different place. That's the point: you don't need to know everything about the month. You need to know where to go on Tuesday.

## Why only three

Thirty reports don't get opened because nobody knows which one matters this week. Three numbers fit on one line in a notebook. You can read them in the time it takes to finish a coffee.

And each of them is something you can act on this week: more people in the door, a better trial, a call to a family that's slipping away.

## Why Monday

Monday is early enough to change the week. A bad number on Monday is a problem you can still fix. The same number at the end of the month is just a report about what already happened.

One line in a notebook. Fifteen minutes. And you know where to go on Tuesday.

## If you track only one

If you run a center and track only one thing this week, track number 3.

Families rarely announce that they're leaving. Two absences in a row is usually the only warning you get — and it's the one number where acting on Monday can still keep a family.
