Owners of learning centers know the feeling. Renewal day comes — and a family you thought was happy just… doesn't renew.

Here's what I learned running coding classes for years.

Parents almost never call to say "we're leaving." They miss one class. Then another. Then: "We'll be back after the holidays." By the time you notice, the decision is already made.

The signal was there the whole time: two absences in a row.

## Why two, and why in a row

One missed class tells you very little. Kids get sick. Families travel. Schedules collide.

Two in a row is different. It's the moment a habit starts to break. The child is out of the rhythm of the week, the parent has found something else to do with that hour, and coming back now takes an effort.

That's why it's the moment to act. Not at renewal. Not when the card declines. When the second class is missed.

## So we turned it into a Monday routine

Every Monday: pull the list of every student who missed two classes in a row.

Send one message to each parent.

Not "we miss you" — that's about us.

Something like this:

> "We missed Max on Tuesday — everything okay? Here's what we did, so he's not behind on Thursday."

It's about the kid. That's why parents answer.

## What makes the message work

The message has three parts.

1. **We noticed — by name, by day.** "We missed Max on Tuesday." It shows someone is paying attention to this child, not sending a mass reminder.
2. **A question that's easy to answer.** "Everything okay?" A parent can reply in two words from the car.
3. **What he missed and how he catches up.** It gives the family a reason to come back on Thursday — and makes coming back easy.

No "please come back" anywhere. The moment the message is about your enrollment, it stops being about their child.

## Fifteen minutes on Monday

That's the whole system. One list, one message per family, the same day every week.

It doesn't need software to start. It needs a fixed time on the calendar and one person who owns it — whoever runs the front desk on Monday morning.
